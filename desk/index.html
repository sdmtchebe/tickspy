Estimated volume profile

Approximate volume distribution from the last 90 candles. Amber marks the largest estimated bucket (POC); brighter buckets form the 70% estimated value area. The dashed line marks the last price.

?

This chart groups volume by an estimated price instead of by time. Each candle's entire volume is assigned to its typical price: (high + low + close) / 3. POC means point of control, the bucket receiving the most volume under that approximation. The value area collects about 70% of the assigned volume. Candle data cannot tell us the actual volume traded at every price, so this is not an order-flow or tick-by-tick profile. Nor is any one feed a complete consolidated tape: in live mode the source covers IEX only, which excludes trading on other exchanges.

Volatility estimate

Open this tab to run the two-stage estimate: Garman-Klass realized volatility with a HAR / GARCH(1,1) filter, then an LSTM residual correction. The network is trained on the loaded history, so the first run takes a minute or two and shows its progress. That training runs in a background worker, so the rest of the desk stays responsive while it works. Nothing needs to be installed or running.

Estimates only, not advice. These are statistical estimates from historical prices. They are not investment advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell, carry no guarantee, and can be wrong. The LSTM is only allowed to influence the estimate in proportion to the accuracy it actually demonstrated out of sample.

Economic calendar USD releases · one week open, the rest one press away

One whole week is always open, Sunday to Saturday in New York time, with every event on it; the weeks after it come from Nasdaq's public calendar. Each further week is one press away. Days with nothing are left out rather than padded, and a week neither source has published yet is not invented either - it fills itself in, because the desk re-reads the calendar every hour and again when the New York day rolls over. Anything the provider has not rated yet is marked not rated and cannot trigger impact-based alerts, so nothing is quietly treated as unimportant.

Arithmetic only: divides a chosen dollar amount by the distance between the two prices you enter.

The total size of the account the trade is being sized against, in dollars. It is used only to turn the risk percentage into a dollar amount; the calculator places no trade.

?

How much of the account you are willing to lose if the stop is hit, as a percent of the whole account. This is a number you choose; it is not a recommendation.

The price you expect to enter the trade at. The distance from here to the stop is the per-share risk the position size is built from.

The price at which you would exit to cap the loss. It must differ from the entry: a wider stop means fewer shares for the same dollar risk.

Backtester

Replays an EMA 9/21 crossover on the loaded bars, long above and short below. One short window and no costs: a sanity check, not proof.

?

The rule is fixed and simple: go long when the 9-period EMA crosses above the 21-period EMA, go short when it crosses below, and flip on the next opposite cross. It runs only on the bars currently loaded, with no costs, no slippage and no position sizing, so it says whether the rule was consistent in this window - not whether it makes money.
